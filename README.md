## Maker vs taker: why the same 0.1% doesn't always cost the same

Maker fees apply when your order adds liquidity — it lands in the order book and waits. Taker fees apply when your order removes liquidity by matching something that's already there. Market orders are always takers. Aggressive limit orders that cross the spread are takers too, even though you clicked "Limit."

Here's the detail that trips people up: **on Gate, maker and taker rates are identical from VIP 0 through VIP 3.** The split only opens at VIP 4. So if you're on a fresh account and someone tells you to "just use limit orders to save on fees," they're wrong at your tier — a resting order costs you exactly what a market order costs until you climb four levels.

From VIP 4 upward the gap is real but narrow. At VIP 5, standard maker is 0.090% against 0.095% taker. At VIP 9 it's 0.070% versus 0.075%. That's half a basis point. Worth having, not worth missing your entry price over.

If you specifically need maker-only execution, Gate's API exposes a post-only order type (internally `PendingOrCancelled`) that prevents your order from crossing the spread and executing as a taker. The tradeoff is obvious: your order might simply never fill.

## How to drop a tier without rewriting your strategy

Gate assigns your level on the better of multiple qualification paths, not just volume:

- **30-day trading volume.** Spot and Convert count at 100%. Perpetual futures count at a lower weighting, and options and CFDs lower still — so a futures-heavy account doesn't climb the spot ladder dollar-for-dollar.
- **Average GT holdings over a 14-day window.** Hold the platform token and you can qualify without trading up to a level.
- **VIP upgrade asset value**, a third path published on the fee page.

Two things soften the whole thing. First, if you qualify by volume and your activity drops off, you get **60 days of downgrade protection** before the level actually comes down. Second, GT fee deduction is a toggle in account settings — the rate shown in the right-hand column of the table above only applies if you've turned it on and hold GT to pay with.

Where the token stops helping is VIP 10. At that point the GT and standard columns converge, so stacking GT for a fee discount is pointless at the tiers where fees are lowest anyway.

## The five fields on the spot order screen

Gate's spot ticket hasn't changed much conceptually: pick your pair, pick your order type, enter price and amount, confirm. The pair naming convention is inverted from what beginners expect — **BTC/USDT means you're trading BTC, priced in USDT.**

Newer users should start with two order types. Market orders prioritize getting filled; you take whatever price the book gives you. Limit orders prioritize price; you may never get filled. Everything else on the platform is a refinement:

- **Stop-limit** — activates a limit order once a trigger price is hit.
- **TP/SL** — automatic exits at preset profit or loss levels, placed after the limit order fills.
- **Iceberg** — shows only part of a large order on the book.
- **TWAP** — spreads execution over time instead of hitting the book all at once.
- **Recurring and trailing orders** — repeating buys and adaptive trigger behavior.

After you submit, check three tabs, and know what each one holds: **Open Orders** (unfilled, cancellable), **Order History** (your instructions), and **Trade History** (actual fills). The last one matters most, because a single order can fill across several trades at several prices. The price you clicked and the price you got are not always the same number.

The order confirmation screen shows the full fee before you commit. Read it once, early, so you know what your tier actually looks like in practice rather than in theory.

## The cost that isn't in any fee table: spread and slippage

Gate's headline advantage is breadth. Published figures for its spot catalogue range from "over 780 coins" in older Gate documentation up to **4,600+ assets** on CoinMarketCap's Gate exchange page, with third-party reviews commonly citing **3,800+ tokens across 7,200+ spot pairs**. CoinMarketCap has also shown Gate's 24-hour spot volume in the region of **$52 billion**, ranking it near the top globally by that measure.

Broad coverage cuts both ways. A token that only trades on Gate may have a wide spread and a thin book outside peak hours. If you send a $5,000 market order into a pair with $20,000 of displayed depth, you'll walk up the book and your average fill will be meaningfully worse than the price you saw. The fee you paid might be 0.1%; the slippage might be 1%.

The practical habit: check order book depth before any market order larger than the top few levels can absorb, and prefer limit orders when price matters more than speed. Total cost is fee plus spread plus slippage plus the cost of not getting filled at all. Optimizing only the first term is how people convince themselves they're saving money while losing it elsewhere.
